    Incident report path

    Source pointers: S17 §18 · G3 §18 · General Circular No. 6/2024

    Use your departmental procedure first. These reminders match G3 §18.1 as a learning aid. Check the current PDF and circular before you quote a clock time in a real event.

    What

    An information security incident is an event you are reasonably sure has harmed confidentiality, integrity, or availability, or has compromised operations. You do not need a finished investigation to report.

    Who, in order

    1. You, as soon as you see it: line manager and the departmental ISIRT contact named in the reporting procedure. Use the after-hours number if the office is shut.
    2. Departmental ISIRT: phone GIRO Standing Office within 60 minutes of becoming aware, then send the preliminary reporting form within 48 hours.
    3. ISIRT Commander: the person who may share, or authorise sharing, outside the handling team.
    4. If critical e-government services, significant implications, or media interest are involved, share scope, impact, containment, and the line-to-take with GIRO Standing Office as those facts exist.
    5. If the case also goes to the Police, the PCPD, or the media, tell GIRO Standing Office.
    6. Where General Circular No. 6/2024 applies, the project person-in-charge or the DITSO reports to the Director of Bureau on that circular’s timetable, and the full report goes on to DPO. Follow the circular’s own site.
    7. A personal-data breach is also reported to the PCPD through their data-breach notification route.

    After the dust settles

    • A post-incident report should reach GIRO Standing Office no later than one week after resolution. Longer investigations use interim reports.
    • Log events, actions, persons, and external correspondence.
    • The practice guide for information security incident handling lives on ITG InfoStation. This hub does not host it.
    A drill uses fake data and is labelled a drill. Do not invent a live incident to finish a walkthrough.

    Learning aid only. Authoritative text remains S17, G3, Security Regulations, and B/D local policies.
