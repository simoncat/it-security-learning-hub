    Who is accountable?

    Accountability is a framework, not a single name. Know the DITSO, the security unit, the information owner, and your own line.

    Not every-word — S17 first, then G3; deep-read Shall and App C; Should = implement whenever possible.

    Reading order: S17, then the same-theme G3 section, then Appendix C if the system is Tier 2 or Tier 3.

    ShallDEC-ACCOUNT-01

    Who leads IT security accountability in my department?

    The Head of the B/D appoints a Departmental IT Security Officer. The DITSO steers IT security. Day-to-day work also sits with the IT security management unit, information owners, and administrators.

    Relevance
    New joiners learning the reporting procedure, and anyone unsure who accepts a risk.
    Applicability
    Roles: All roles.Stages: Any stage.Tiers: Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3.
    Prerequisites
    You are inside a B/D, or you support one. Names come from your unit, not from this hub.
    Implementation guidance
    Find the DITSO, the security unit, the ISIRT contact, and your line manager in the departmental reporting procedure. Leave a blank if you do not know. Do not invent a name.
    Source
    S17 §5 G3 §5

    ShallDEC-ACCOUNT-02

    What remains my job if I am not the DITSO?

    Every staff member has duties for the access, information, and systems they use. A specialist role adds duties. It does not move your own Shall items onto someone else.

    Relevance
    Elevated users, administrators, developers, and management support.
    Applicability
    Roles: All five roles.Stages: Any stage.Tiers: Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3.
    Prerequisites
    You know which role description is closest to the work you actually do.
    Implementation guidance
    Open your role path and the theme decision aid (access, operations, development, incident). Check the reporting procedure so you know who carries the rest.
    Source
    S17 §5 G3 §5 S17 §9

    ShallDEC-ACCOUNT-03

    Who accepts a risk that is still open?

    Someone in management owns the risk and accepts what remains after treatment. An administrator or developer can describe the risk. They do not silently accept it.

    Relevance
    Project and operations staff preparing a residual-risk note.
    Applicability
    Roles: Management support, security unit, and information owners. Others contribute the facts.Stages: 01 Project initiation, 02 Design, 06 Go-live, 08 Change.Tiers: Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3.
    Prerequisites
    The risk is described: what can go wrong, and what is already in place.
    Implementation guidance
    Write the risk in the department’s own register or paper. Name the owner. Open S17 §7 and G3 §7 before you treat a verbal “it is fine” as acceptance.
    Source
    S17 §7 G3 §7
