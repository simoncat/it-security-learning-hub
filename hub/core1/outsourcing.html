    Outsourcing and suppliers

    A supplier does not absorb your Shall. There is no Appendix C block on §17. The outsourcing section itself is the extra care.

    Not every-word — S17 first, then G3; deep-read Shall and App C; Should = implement whenever possible.

    Reading order: S17, then the same-theme G3 section, then Appendix C if the system is Tier 2 or Tier 3.

    Legend

    ShallDEC-OUTSRC-01

    If a vendor runs it, do our Shall items disappear?

    No. The B/D remains responsible for government information. The contract has to make the supplier’s part explicit, and you still own the part that stays with you.

    Relevance
    Contract managers, project staff, and administrators of hosted systems.
    Applicability
    Roles: System / LAN administrator, Application developer, IT security administrator / unit, and Management support.Stages: 03 Procurement, 07 Operations.Tiers: Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3.
    Prerequisites
    A supplier, cloud service, or contractor is in the path.
    Implementation guidance
    Write the split: what the supplier does, what the B/D still does, and how you will know. Open S17 §17 and G3 §17 before tender award, not after an incident.
    Source
    S17 §17 G3 §17

    ShouldDEC-OUTSRC-02

    What should I ask before I send data to a supplier?

    Where it will be, who can see it, how incidents are reported, and how it comes back or is destroyed at the end.

    Relevance
    Project staff and information owners.
    Applicability
    Roles: Application developer, IT security administrator / unit, and Management support.Stages: 03 Procurement, 09 Decommissioning.Tiers: Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3.
    Prerequisites
    You know the classification of the data.
    Implementation guidance
    Use the local outsourcing checklist if you have one. If you do not, the questions above are the minimum conversation with the security unit.
    Source
    S17 §17 G3 §17 S17 §10
