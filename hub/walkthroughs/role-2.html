    System / LAN administrator — privileged access, request to review

    Source pointers: G3 §11 · Appendix C §11 and §14

    Not every-word — S17 first, then G3; deep-read Shall and App C; Should = implement whenever possible.

    Reading order: S17, then the same-theme G3 section, then Appendix C if the system is Tier 2 or Tier 3.

    Where a step is Should, you may defer it if you record why. Shall steps are the ones the policy treats as mandatory. App C steps apply when the system is Tier 2 or Tier 3.

    1. Step 1. Name one production system

      Write the system name and the tier if it has already been confirmed. If not, write “tier not confirmed”.

      Artefacts to keep: System name.

    2. Step 2. Describe the privilege

      What is requested, for whom, and why a lesser right is not enough.

      Artefacts to keep: The request text.

      Shall: least privilege.

    3. Step 3. Check password and MFA expectations

      Note the strong-password rule. If the system is Tier 2 or 3, note MFA for interactive privileged logon where feasible.

      Artefacts to keep: A yes/no on MFA, with the reason if it is not feasible.

    4. Step 4. Name the approver

      Who grants, who is informed, and where the ticket will live. Do not grant anything as part of this learning note.

      Artefacts to keep: Approver and ticket location.

    5. Step 5. Decide drill or real work

      If this is not an authorised live request, mark the walkthrough “tabletop — not executed”. Execute a grant only when it is your real job and you are allowed to.

      Artefacts to keep: The tabletop or the real ticket number.

    6. Step 6. Diary the independent check

      For Tier 2/3, an independent party reviews privileged use at least every six months. Put the next date in your own calendar.

      Artefacts to keep: Next review date.

      App C Shall when the tier is 2 or 3.

    7. Step 7. Gather artefacts

      Request, approval, ticket, and review date. No passwords in the note.

      Artefacts to keep: Those four pointers.

    8. Step 8. Note a deferral

      If a Should item waits (for example a nicer inventory tool), write why and a date to revisit.

      Artefacts to keep: The deferral line.

    Alternative exercise

    Alternative: one host’s patch or vulnerability item. Record the advisory, the decision, the date, and whether DITSO must be told if it will pass a month.
