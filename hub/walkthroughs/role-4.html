    IT security administrator / unit — privileged-account review sample

    Source pointers: G3 §5.3.1 · G3 §11 · Appendix C §11

    Not every-word — S17 first, then G3; deep-read Shall and App C; Should = implement whenever possible.

    Reading order: S17, then the same-theme G3 section, then Appendix C if the system is Tier 2 or Tier 3.

    Where a step is Should, you may defer it if you record why. Shall steps are the ones the policy treats as mandatory. App C steps apply when the system is Tier 2 or Tier 3.

    1. Step 1. Choose a sample of accounts

      Five privileged accounts on one system, or fewer if that is all there are. Use identifiers your policy allows in a working note.

      Artefacts to keep: The sample list.

    2. Step 2. Check the purpose

      Each account still has a job. Orphaned and shared accounts are findings.

      Artefacts to keep: A column: keep, change, or remove.

      Shall: least privilege.

    3. Step 3. Check independence

      For Tier 2/3 the reviewer is not the person whose use is under review. Say who the independent party is.

      Artefacts to keep: Reviewer name or role.

      App C Shall at least every six months.

    4. Step 4. Look at log integrity

      Could the account holder edit the log of their own use? If yes, that is a finding.

      Artefacts to keep: Yes/no and the control.

    5. Step 5. MFA and passwords

      Interactive privileged logon: is MFA there where feasible? Is the strong-password policy actually enforced?

      Artefacts to keep: Two yes/no answers.

    6. Step 6. Joiner-mover-leaver spot check

      Pick one recent leaver. Was privileged access removed?

      Artefacts to keep: The example, with no extra personal data.

    7. Step 7. Write findings as gaps

      Control, evidence, gap, owner, due. Use the gap template if you want a table.

      Artefacts to keep: The gap rows.

    8. Step 8. Do not collapse roles

      If the same person is both security admin and system admin, record that against the Should on segregation. Do not “fix” it by sharing a password.

      Artefacts to keep: The segregation note.

      Should: separate the seats whenever possible.

    Alternative exercise

    Alternative: an alert drill. Pick a past benign alert or a vendor advisory. Triage it on paper: what it might mean, who you would call, and what you would not do (do not probe systems you do not run).

    Back to apply and close gaps
