    Elevated end user — report-path tabletop

    Source pointers: G3 §18 · G3 §11 · G3 §9

    Not every-word — S17 first, then G3; deep-read Shall and App C; Should = implement whenever possible.

    Reading order: S17, then the same-theme G3 section, then Appendix C if the system is Tier 2 or Tier 3.

    Where a step is Should, you may defer it if you record why. Shall steps are the ones the policy treats as mandatory. App C steps apply when the system is Tier 2 or Tier 3.

    1. Step 1. Label it a drill

      Pick a plausible story, such as a colleague opening a suspicious message. Write DRILL on the note. Do not use a real person’s mail or a live attachment.

      Artefacts to keep: A one-line scenario.

      Shall: do not turn a drill into a real incident report.

    2. Step 2. Write the facts you would report

      What happened, when, which system, and whether classified information or personal data might be involved. Unknown is an acceptable answer.

      Artefacts to keep: A five-line fact note.

    3. Step 3. Name who you would call

      From the reporting procedure, name the line manager, the ISIRT contact, and the after-hours number. If you do not know one, that is a gap to fill with your unit, not to invent.

      Artefacts to keep: The names, or a list of blanks.

    4. Step 4. Walk the departmental steps on paper

      Follow the published local procedure. Remember G3 §18: report once harm is reasonably apparent. The ISIRT’s clock toward GIRO is theirs to run.

      Artefacts to keep: The step list from the local procedure.

      Shall: use the real path for a real event.

    5. Step 5. Note who may speak outside the team

      Sharing beyond the handlers is for the ISIRT Commander to authorise. Your drill note should say you would not post details in a wide chat.

      Artefacts to keep: One sentence on sharing.

    6. Step 6. List the artefacts a real event would keep

      Timeline, who was told, form or ticket reference, and what was preserved. For the drill, keep only the informal note.

      Artefacts to keep: The informal drill note.

      Should: a personal copy. Do not build a second official record.

    7. Step 7. Separate Shall from Should

      Reporting and preserving evidence as the procedure requires are Shall. Extra tidy-ups you postpone are Should, with a reason.

      Artefacts to keep: Two short lists.

      Record any Should you defer, and why.

    8. Step 8. Flag the tier question

      If the story touched a system that might be Tier 2 or 3, mark App C for a later read. Do not declare the tier yourself.

      Artefacts to keep: A question for the Information Owner.

    Alternative exercise

    Alternative: a joiner or leaver checklist for an elevated account. List the account, the approver, the day access starts or stops, and where the ticket lives. Do not perform a real removal unless it is your authorised work.
