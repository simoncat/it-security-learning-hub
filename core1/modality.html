Shall, Should, and May

Read the modality before the control. Shall comes first. You do not have to read every word of S17 and G3.

Not every-word — S17 first, then G3; deep-read Shall and App C; Should = implement whenever possible.

Reading order: S17, then the same-theme G3 section, then Appendix C if the system is Tier 2 or Tier 3.

ShallDEC-MOD-01

Does this line say Shall?

Shall indicates a mandatory requirement.

Relevance
Anyone deciding whether a control can wait.
Applicability
Roles: All roles.Stages: Any stage.Tiers: Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3.
Prerequisites
You are looking at sections or clauses in S17/G3.
Implementation guidance
Mark the line Shall, do it, and keep records to evidence compliance with security measures. Refer to same-numbered G3 section for implementation.
Source
S17 §4 G3 §4

ShouldDEC-MOD-02

What do I do with Should?

Should indicates best practice, which should be implemented whenever possible. If you defer it, document the reason for reviewing in future.

Relevance
Staff choosing what to schedule after the mandatory items.
Applicability
Roles: All roles.Stages: Any stage.Tiers: Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3.
Prerequisites
The Shall items for the same theme are already identified.
Implementation guidance
Implement the control whenever possible. A deferral needs a reason.
Source
S17 §4 G3 §4

MayDEC-MOD-03

Can I treat May as optional requirement?

May is a desirable best practice. Use it when it helps.

Relevance
Anyone tempted to read May as a waiver.
Applicability
Roles: All roles.Stages: Any stage.Tiers: Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3.
Prerequisites
You have already read through those shall and should clauses.
Implementation guidance
Apply May where it reduces risk for your system.
Source
S17 §4 G3 §4
